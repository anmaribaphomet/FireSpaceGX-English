PSI-68 · FLEX-2 (ISS, MDCA): droplet combustion. Its keywords include n-decane and ethanol, and 50/50 mixtures of n-heptane/ethanol and n-hexanol/n-decane were tested in the FLEX-2 configuration.

The compound changes how much soot the flame forms and, therefore, its visible color. The trend is qualitative (alkanes form more soot than alcohols); in this model the temperature does not depend on the compound. FLEX-2 tested O₂ from 10 to 40 % and pressures from 0.5 to 3 atm.

Scale

Measurements are in cm (approx.): 1 chart unit = 1 cm. The scale is anchored to the burner diameter you choose (0.6 cm by default): with size 5, 1 g and 21 % oxygen the flame is ~8 cm tall and ~3 cm wide, like a small lab flame. If you set a burner twice as wide, the flame is twice as tall and wide; if it is half, the flame is half.

How is the color calculated?

What glows yellow/orange in a flame is incandescent soot, which emits like a black body: its color is calculated with Planck's law and the CIE 1931 color matching functions for the temperature of each point, and the side bar shows those same colors over the temperature range of your flame. The blue is the emission of the CH* (~431 nm) and C₂* (~516 nm) radicals in the reaction zone, and it only dominates when the fuel forms little soot, like the methanol in FLEX ("very dim blue flame"). Brightness on screen is adjusted the way a camera would; the hue is not changed.
